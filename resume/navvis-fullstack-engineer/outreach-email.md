## Notes on this draft

**The "where I'm strong / where I'm not" structure is the point.** It is unusual enough to be memorable and it does something useful: by volunteering the gap you control how it is framed, and you get to supply the transfer argument before anyone decides you do not have one. Hiding it means the reviewer forms that judgement without you in the room.

**Delete the bracketed sentence unless it is true.** It is the single strongest line in the email, so it is also the most damaging one to fake. A real Spring Boot repository you can link takes a weekend — see the project described in [`TAILORING-NOTES.md`](TAILORING-NOTES.md) — and it changes this email from a request for the benefit of the doubt into a demonstration.

**Do not apologise for the gap, and do not over-explain it.** One paragraph, stated flatly, then move on to the 3D work. The tone you want is a competent engineer assessing their own fit, not a candidate hoping to be let through.

**The WebXR paragraph is your differentiator, so keep it last and keep it short.** Most applicants for this role will have Java and no 3D web experience at all. You have the inverse. Say it once and let it sit — and be precise if it comes up in the call: you integrated WebXR components and optimized the payload around them, you did not write a renderer.

**Relocation is already solved for you.** The posting offers full visa and relocation support outright, so unlike the Berlin applications there is nothing to negotiate or pre-empt. One sentence confirming you are willing is enough.
