# Pharmacovigilance Switch Playbook

Situation: M.Pharm (Pharmacology) completed 2026, certified in Clinical Research & Pharmacovigilance,
currently an R&D apprentice in Analytical R&D at Dr. Reddy's Laboratories, wanting to move into a
core pharmacovigilance / drug safety role.

That situation is stronger than a plain fresher profile and weaker than a PV-experienced one, so the
strategy has to play to the strength: **she is already inside a large regulated pharma company and
already has PV training.** Almost everything below is ordered by probability of a shortlist, not by
effort.

## 1. The highest-probability route: internal move inside Dr. Reddy's

Dr. Reddy's runs its own global pharmacovigilance / drug safety function. An internal transfer is
easier than an external switch because the company already has her documentation, references and
compliance record, and internal postings usually waive the "1–2 years PV experience" filter.

Concrete steps:

1. Find the internal job portal listing for Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety / Medical Safety and
   apply with the new resume (the version with the headline matching their internal title).
2. Ask the current AR&D supervisor for a referral or at least an informational introduction to the PV
   department head. Frame it as: *"I completed M.Pharm in Pharmacology with pharmacovigilance
   specialisation and a PV certification; I want to build my career in drug safety inside Dr. Reddy's
   and would value 15 minutes to understand how the team works and what they look for."*
3. Ask HR directly whether an internal transfer is possible after completing a defined tenure in the
   apprenticeship (in most Indian pharma companies the answer is 6–12 months) and get the timeline in
   writing, so the external search can be paced against it.
4. Volunteer for anything adjacent: complaint handling, product quality complaints, deviation
   documentation, literature review support. Any documented PV-adjacent task becomes a resume bullet.

## 2. External targets, tiered

**Tier 1 — CROs / BPOs that hire PV freshers in volume and train on Argus:** IQVIA, Accenture,
Cognizant, ICON, Parexel, Fortrea (ex-Covance/Sciformix), Syneos Health, Indegene, TCS Life Sciences,
Wipro Life Sciences, Navitas Life Sciences, APCER Life Sciences, PharmaLex, Freyr, Clarivate,
Lifebit/Bioclinica-type safety units, Tech Mahindra Life Sciences.

**Tier 2 — Indian pharma with in-house PV teams:** Dr. Reddy's, Sun Pharma, Cipla, Lupin, Zydus,
Torrent, Alkem, Glenmark, Aurobindo, Intas, Mankind, Hetero, MSN, Biocon.

**Tier 3 — MNC pharma (harder for freshers, worth applying anyway):** Novartis (Hyderabad), Sanofi,
GSK, Pfizer, AstraZeneca, Amgen, Bayer, Novo Nordisk — these hire into Hyderabad/Bengaluru global
capability centres and occasionally open "associate" PV roles to 0–1 year candidates.

Best-hiring cities: Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Chennai, Pune, Mumbai, Noida/Gurugram, Kochi, Trivandrum,
Ahmedabad. Applying with "open to relocation" stated on the resume materially widens the funnel.

Search strings to use on Naukri / LinkedIn / company career pages:
`drug safety associate`, `pharmacovigilance associate`, `safety associate trainee`,
`ICSR case processing`, `PV associate fresher`, `drug safety specialist`, `case processing associate`,
`aggregate reporting associate`, `literature screening pharmacovigilance`, `PV operations`.

## 5. Close the three real gaps (in order of return)

1. **Hands-on Argus Safety.** Nearly every JD names it. A paid instructor-led Argus case-entry course
   with database access converts `Argus (foundational training)` into `Argus Safety (trained,
   hands-on case entry)` — the single biggest keyword upgrade available. Add the certificate to the
   resume and to the Safety Databases line the day it is completed.
2. **MedDRA depth.** Work through the free MedDRA MSSO introductory materials and the *MedDRA Term
   Selection: Points to Consider* document; be ready to code 5 sample verbatims on the spot in an
   interview.
3. **Practice narratives.** Write 10 practice narratives from published case reports (patient
   history → suspect drug and dates → event chronology → treatment → outcome → reporter causality)
   and keep them as a portfolio to offer if an interviewer asks for a writing sample. Also keep a
   count of the ICSRs processed during certification training — that number belongs in the `[N]`
   placeholder in the resume.

Optional, once employed in PV: DIA / ISoP membership, RAC (Regulatory Affairs Certification) later
for a signal/risk-management track.

## 6. Interview prep — the questions that actually get asked

**The switch question (always asked, prepare a 30-second answer):**
*"Why move from analytical R&D to pharmacovigilance?"* → "My M.Pharm was in Pharmacology with
pharmacovigilance and drug safety coursework, and I certified in Clinical Research and
Pharmacovigilance in 2025 — that is the field I trained for. The AR&D apprenticeship gave me
something valuable for PV: GMP-level documentation discipline, ALCOA+ data integrity, SOP compliance
and the habit of escalating anything discrepant. In drug safety, accuracy and audit-ready
documentation under deadline is the job, so the discipline transfers directly. I want to apply it to
patient safety rather than product testing."

**Technical checklist to be fluent on:**

| Topic | Must be able to say |
|---|---|
| AE vs ADR vs SAE | AE = any untoward medical occurrence, no causality implied; ADR = causal relationship suspected; SAE = meets one of the six seriousness criteria |
| Six seriousness criteria | Death; life-threatening; hospitalisation or prolongation; persistent/significant disability or incapacity; congenital anomaly/birth defect; other medically significant event |
| Four case validity criteria | Identifiable patient, identifiable reporter, suspect medicinal product, adverse event |
| MedDRA hierarchy | SOC → HLGT → HLT → PT → LLT; code at LLT, report at PT; term selection per MTS:PTC |
| WHO-DD | Drug dictionary for suspect/concomitant product coding |
| WHO-UMC categories | Certain, probable/likely, possible, unlikely, conditional/unclassified, unassessable |
| Naranjo scale | ≥9 definite, 5–8 probable, 1–4 possible, ≤0 doubtful |
| Expectedness / listedness | Event compared against reference safety information (IB, SmPC, package insert) |
| SUSAR | Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reaction — expedited reporting in clinical trials |
| Case flow | Intake → duplicate check → triage → data entry → coding → causality/seriousness → narrative → medical review → QC → submission → follow-up → archival |
| Global timelines | 7 calendar days for fatal/life-threatening expedited reports, 15 calendar days for other serious; non-serious per local rules |
| India (NDCT Rules 2019 / PvPI) | 7 days fatal or life-threatening SAE, 15 days other serious, 90 days non-serious AE; PSUR per prescribed periodicity; MedDRA now used for ICSR coding per PvPI guidance |
| Regulatory documents | CIOMS I, MedWatch 3500A, E2B(R3) XML, line listings |
| Aggregate reports | PSUR/PBRER (ICH E2C(R2)), DSUR (ICH E2F), ACO/addendum reports |
| Signal management | Signal detection → validation → assessment → recommendation; disproportionality basics |
| Risk documents | RMP (safety concerns, pharmacovigilance plan, risk minimisation measures), PSMF |
| Special situations | Pregnancy/lactation exposure, overdose, off-label use, medication error, abuse/misuse, lack of efficacy |
| Quality | Good Documentation Practice, ALCOA+, self and peer QC, deviations/CAPA, inspection readiness |

**Behavioural answers to have ready:** an error you caught in documentation; how you handle a
deadline conflict; comfort with rotational/night shifts (say yes clearly — it is a gating question);
how you handle confidential patient data.
